# Job to Be Done: File Taxes Without Stress

**Job Title (Ultimate Practical Outcome):**
Know your tax liability year-round so that filing season is a formality, not a crisis.

---

## Functional Job

To track income, estimate quarterly tax obligations, and identify deductions on an ongoing basis—so that filing taxes requires no scramble, produces no surprises, and doesn't require a last-minute accountant panic session.

---

## Emotional & Social Jobs

### Emotional Needs
- **Feel prepared**: Know your approximate tax bill before April without waiting for a CPA
- **Reduce dread**: Stop treating tax season as a once-a-year emergency
- **Avoid shock**: Never again open a tax return to find an unexpected $20K liability
- **Feel competent**: Understand what you owe and why—not just blindly pay what someone tells you

### Social Needs
- **Appear responsible**: Be the business owner who has their finances in order, not the one scrambling in March
- **Maintain CPA relationship**: Show up to your accountant with organized records, not a shoebox of receipts
- **Avoid penalties**: Not paying quarterly estimates reflects poorly on your financial discipline

---

## Circumstances

### When the Job Emerges
- **Every quarter** (estimated tax payments due mid-January, April, June, September)
- **When income spikes unexpectedly** (landed a big project, sold a product batch, earned a commission)
- **January–April** (W-2s, 1099s arrive; filing deadline looms)
- **When making a big business decision** (hiring a contractor, buying equipment—"will this affect my taxes?")
- **After a bad year** (income dropped; overpaid estimates; want to understand what happened)

### Constraints
- **Variable income makes estimation hard**: A solopreneur who earns $6K in January and $40K in February can't use static monthly estimates
- **Multiple income streams add complexity**: Freelance + W-2 job + Substack + consulting = four different tax treatments
- **Deduction knowledge gaps**: Most owners don't know which expenses qualify; they either over-claim (risky) or under-claim (costly)
- **No real-time visibility**: Current tools (QuickBooks, spreadsheets) show income but not estimated tax liability in plain language
- **CPA dependency**: Many owners rely entirely on their accountant for tax intelligence, which means they're always looking backward, never forward

---

## Steps (How People Currently Solve It, Solution-Independent)

1. **Trigger**: Q payment due date approaching, or accountant sends an email reminder
2. **Estimate**: Look at last year's tax bill and divide by 4 (static method—ignores income changes)
3. **Pay**: Log into IRS Direct Pay and submit estimated payment, often without knowing if the amount is right
4. **Forget**: Return to running the business; no visibility into cumulative liability
5. **Reconcile (once a year)**: In January, gather all 1099s, bank statements, and expense receipts
6. **Scramble**: Sort through months of transactions trying to remember which were business expenses
7. **Hand off**: Send disorganized records to a CPA and wait 4–8 weeks for a return
8. **Get surprised**: Learn in March or April what the actual bill is—often higher than expected
9. **Pay**: Wire payment; vow to "do it better next year"; repeat

## Needs (How People Would Describe a Job Well Done)

### Core Needs
- **Show me my estimated tax liability right now**, updated as income comes in (not just at filing time)
- **Calculate quarterly payments dynamically**—if I earn more in Q3, tell me to pay more in Q3
- **Flag deductible expenses automatically** so I'm not guessing what I can write off
- **Summarize my year-to-date tax position in plain English**: "Based on your income so far, you owe approximately $14,200 for 2024"
- **Tell me how a decision affects my taxes** ("If you hire a contractor at $3K/month, your taxable income drops by ~$36K/year")

### Emotional Needs
- **Remove the annual dread** by making taxes a background process, not a yearly event
- **Give me confidence that I'm not under- or overpaying** each quarter
- **Make me feel like a competent business owner**, not someone who needs to be rescued by their CPA every April

### Data & Visibility Needs
- **Show me effective tax rate vs. marginal rate** so I understand what growth actually costs me
- **Track deductions I've already captured** and surface ones I might be missing
- **Show me year-over-year comparison**: "Last year at this point, you had paid $X; this year you've paid $Y"
- **Connect tax liability to cashflow**: "Your next quarterly payment is due in 47 days; you have $22K in the bank, so this is manageable"

## Customer Evidence

This job is validated across **10 interviews** in the Tax & Compliance PM area:

- **9 of 10** described tax prep as "stressful," "chaotic," or something they actively avoid thinking about
- **5 of 10** reported being surprised by their tax bill at filing time—amounts ranged from $4K to $40K unexpected
- **8 of 10** use the prior year's return as their only method for estimating quarterly payments
- **7 of 10** said they believe they're missing deductions but don't know which ones or how much it costs them
- **4 of 10** have been penalized for underpayment at least once in the past three years
