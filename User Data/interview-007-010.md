# Interview 007: Amara Okafor

**Date**: February 15, 2024 | **Duration**: 49 minutes  
**Business**: Social Media Management (freelance) | **Status**: Non-user  
**Annual Revenue**: ~$55K | **Company Size**: Solo | **Years in Business**: 2

---

## The Problem: Recurring Client Automation

Amara manages social media for 12 recurring clients (small businesses, mostly local). All revenue is recurring monthly, but she has no formal invoicing system. She sends Stripe links manually each month.

**Amara's pain**: "I have the same 12 clients month after month. I should be invoicing automatically, but I manually send Stripe links each month. It's inefficient."

### Current Process
1. Keeps list of 12 clients (names, monthly rates, due dates) in spreadsheet
2. First of each month, manually sends Stripe links to all 12
3. Tracks "paid" status in spreadsheet (manually updated)
4. Follows up with late payers

**Time spent**: 30 minutes/month on invoicing admin that should be automatic = 6 hours/year

---

## What Would Change Behavior

**Amara**: "If I could set up 12 recurring invoices and just forget about them, I'd save 6 hours/year. And I'd invoice on-time every month instead of sometimes forgetting a client."

### Desired Workflow
1. Set up client + monthly rate once
2. System auto-invoices on the 1st of month
3. Client receives invoice automatically
4. Payment tracked automatically
5. Amara gets summary of who paid/hasn't paid

---

## Key Insight: Recurring Revenue Automation

For solopreneurs with recurring clients, automated recurring invoicing is table stakes.

**What Amara needs**:
1. Recurring invoice templates (set up once)
2. Auto-invoice trigger (1st of month, or custom date)
3. Payment tracking (which clients paid?)
4. Late payment alerts
5. Optional: Auto-charge capability (if client authorizes)

---

## Behavioral Evidence

Amara would adopt a tool that:
- ✅ Automates recurring invoicing (removes 30 min/month of manual work)
- ✅ Costs <$30/month (payback in 1 month of time saved)
- ✅ Integrates with Stripe (already uses it)
- ❌ Requires manual setup or ongoing attention

---

**Key takeaway**: Recurring revenue needs automation, not just visibility. Auto-billing is non-negotiable for this segment.

# Interview 008: Michael Torres

**Date**: February 18, 2024 | **Duration**: 58 minutes  
**Business**: Design Studio | **Status**: Non-user (considering)  
**Annual Revenue**: ~$1.1M | **Company Size**: 12 people | **Years in Business**: 7

---

## The Problem: Tool Fragmentation at Scale

Michael's business is complex: print design, web design, branding, consulting. Many projects are retainers; some are project-based. Revenue is stable and growing.

**Michael's problem**: "I use FreshBooks for invoicing, Asana for project management, Quickbooks for accounting, Stripe for payments, and separate time tracking. Everything talks to nothing. I spend hours reconciling."

### Tool Stack Pain
1. Team logs time in time-tracking tool
2. FreshBooks needs manual import of time
3. Asana has project scope (doesn't talk to FreshBooks)
4. Stripe payments come through (manually matched)
5. QB requires manual export from FreshBooks

**Result**: 4-5 hours/week of admin work matching data across systems

---

## Why Michael is Interested in Ledger

**Michael**: "Ledger consolidates invoicing + cashflow visibility. If I could invoice in Ledger, see payments, and integrate QB, I'd switch from FreshBooks."

### What Michael Needs
- Invoicing (project + retainer)
- Payment tracking (what's paid/pending)
- Time tracking integration
- QB integration (system of record)
- Team permissions (different clients for different team members)
- Client payment history (for communication)

---

## The Challenge: Switching Cost

Despite liking Ledger, Michael has invested 7 years in FreshBooks. His data is there; team knows it. Switching requires:
- Exporting 7 years of data
- Re-training team
- Ensuring QB integration works
- Risk of data loss

**Michael**: "I like what Ledger is doing, but I need to see it working a few months before I commit. Switching cost is too high."

---

## Hypothesis Implication

**Ledger needs to address switching cost** for established agencies:
- Better migration tools
- Integration guarantees
- Risk reduction (data backup, rollback)
- Training/onboarding support

---

**Key takeaway**: Consolidation is attractive, but switching cost is real for established customers. Build migration path.

# Interview 009: Priya Sharma

**Annual Revenue**: ~$60K | **Company Size**: Solo | **Years in Business**: 3

---

## The Problem: Professionalism Without Corporate Feeling

Priya is a marketing consultant working with small business owners. She has 6-8 active clients and is growing. Currently zero invoicing system—clients pay via PayPal or bank transfer after she sends an estimate.

**Priya's pain**: "I want to look professional, but I don't want to look like a big corporate firm. I want invoices that feel credible without being intimidating."

### Current Process
1. Sends estimate via email
2. Client approves
3. Priya sends PayPal link or bank transfer instructions
4. No formal invoice

**Impact**:
- Clients don't feel like they're working with a legitimate business
- No audit trail or contract record
- Difficult to follow up on unpaid invoices

---

## The Emotional Barrier

**Priya**: "I worry that if I send a formal invoice, small business owners will think I'm charging them like a big agency. I want to look professional but approachable."

This is similar to Lisa's concern (Interview 005)—fear that professionalism = corporate, which scares away bootstrapped clients.

---

## What Priya Wants

"A simple invoicing tool that looks friendly and approachable, not like accounting software. Something I can send to clients that makes me look credible without being intimidating."

### Specific Needs
1. **Invoice design**: Friendly, not corporate
2. **Simple setup**: Not accountant-level complexity
3. **Stripe integration**: Already uses Stripe
4. **Payment tracking**: Which clients paid?
5. **Professional appearance**: Client sees legitimate business

---

## Hypothesis Validation

**Tone and design are features** for solopreneurs. A "friendly invoice" is different from a "corporate invoice."

**This validates**: Solopreneurs care deeply about how they're perceived. Invoicing tool design (visuals, language, tone) is as important as functionality.

---

**Key takeaway**: For solopreneurs, professionalism ≠ corporate. Design and tone matter.
