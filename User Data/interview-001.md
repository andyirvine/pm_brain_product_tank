# Interview 001: Maya Chen
**Segment**: Solopreneur | **Business**: Freelance Designer | **Status**: Non-user (Wave) | **Date**: Jan 2024

---

## Background

**Business Model**: Project-based design work (branding, web design, social media graphics)  
**Annual Revenue**: ~$85K | **Years in Business**: 4 | **Current Tools**: Wave (accounting), Stripe (payments), Gmail (invoicing)

---

## The Problem

**Primary Pain Point**: Clients often pay late; Maya gets anxious about following up.

**Quote**: "I'll send an invoice and then I just... wait. And worry. I don't want to be that person who nags people for money. It feels rude. So I'll wait an extra two weeks before sending a reminder. By then the invoice is 45 days old."

**Specific Example**: 
- Completed a $3,500 branding project for a client in December
- Sent invoice on Dec 15 (Net 30 terms)
- No payment by Jan 15; Maya felt awkward sending reminder
- Finally sent gentle follow-up on Jan 25
- Client paid on Feb 3 (19 days late)

**Impact**: 
- Cash flow uncertainty affects her ability to invest in tools or take time off
- Often working on next project before last one is paid
- Has had to turn down projects because she couldn't afford to wait for payment

---

## Current Workaround

1. **Invoicing**: Uses Wave to create invoice, downloads PDF, emails manually
2. **Tracking**: Opens Gmail to see when client acknowledges, then checks Stripe dashboard for payment notification
3. **Follow-up**: Manually reminds herself via a note in Asana; feels awkward every time
4. **Reconciliation**: Monthly, spots checks Wave against Stripe to ensure everything is recorded

**Time spent**: ~20 minutes per invoice (including download, email, follow-up)

**Biggest friction**: The gap between invoice sent and payment received feels long. No visibility into "Is the invoice even open? Did they lose it?"

---

## Emotional Job

**What she wants to feel**: Confident that clients will pay, and professional when reminding them.

**Current emotional state**: 
- Anxious ("Am I going to have cash next month?")
- Slightly ashamed ("I hate being the person who has to ask for money")
- Uncertain ("Is this client going to pay at all?")

**Quote**: "If I could send a reminder that didn't feel like I was being a nag, I'd do it immediately. But right now, every reminder feels personal."

---

## Jobs to Be Done

### Primary: Get Paid by My Clients
- **Current success metric**: "Invoice is paid within 45 days"
- **Desired success metric**: "Invoice is paid within 30 days, and I don't feel awkward asking"

### Secondary: Know My Cash Position
- Wants to know: "I have $5,000 in outstanding invoices. $3,000 of it arrives next week."
- Currently: Has to manually check Wave, Stripe, and email to piece together the picture

---

## Current Hypothesis

**Ledger Hypothesis**: "If Maya had automated payment reminders that felt professional (not pushy), she'd send them immediately, reducing her DSO from 45 to 30 days."

**Evidence from this interview**:
- ✅ She mentions automation as desirable
- ✅ She fears looking pushy, but would embrace a solution that removes personalization from the reminder
- ✅ She's willing to try something new (currently satisfied with Wave for accounting, but invoicing is painful)

**Validation status**: Hypothesis is supported by this interview; corroborated in interviews 003, 007, 009.

---

## Related Interview Evidence

**Similar pain**: Interview 003 (Sarah) and 005 (Lisa) both mention payment follow-up anxiety.

**Different context**: Interview 002 (Raj, agency owner) doesn't have this anxiety—suggests segmentation opportunity (larger teams may normalize payment chasing).

---

## Needs & Desired Features

1. **Payment visibility**: Dashboard showing which invoices are paid, pending, overdue
2. **Automated reminders**: System sends reminder if invoice is unpaid after X days (Maya chooses the trigger)
3. **Client context**: Know if client "always pays late" or "pays on time"
4. **Quick invoicing**: 2-minute flow, not 20 minutes
5. **Professional tone**: Reminder should feel business-like, not passive-aggressive

---

## Segment Implications

**Solopreneur-specific insights**:
- No team; Maya is doing invoicing, follow-up, reconciliation alone
- Project-based revenue (variable; no recurring contracts yet)
- Lacks confidence in "business-y" things (still feels like a freelancer, not a business)
- Budget-conscious; willing to pay, but only for tools that clearly save time or reduce stress

**Competitive positioning**: Maya is on Wave because it's free. But she'd switch to Ledger if the invoicing + payment tracking experience clearly reduced her stress (worth $15-30/mo).

## How This Interview Informs Product

**Ledger Product Implications**:
1. ✅ Automated payment reminders are table stakes for invoicing
2. ✅ Emotional tone matters (professional, not pushy)
3. ✅ Solopreneurs need simplicity + speed (2-minute invoicing)
4. ✅ Visibility into DSO trends (which clients pay fast vs. slow) is high-value

**Positioning insight**: Maya doesn't want a "better Wave." She wants a tool that makes her feel like a professional and removes anxiety. This is the emotional angle Ledger owns.
