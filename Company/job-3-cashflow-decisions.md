# Job to Be Done: Make Confident Cashflow Decisions

**Job Title (Ultimate Practical Outcome):**
Make business decisions—hiring, investing, pricing, taking time off—without fear that you're guessing about your finances.

---

## Functional Job

To see a clear, forward-looking picture of cash coming in and going out—by source, by timing, and by scenario—so that decisions about growth, expenses, and risk are grounded in numbers, not instinct.

---

## Emotional & Social Jobs

### Emotional Needs
- **Feel in control**: Know what your business can actually afford before committing to something
- **Reduce paralysis**: Stop defaulting to "no" on every growth decision because you're not sure if you can afford it
- **Build confidence**: Make a hire, take on a new tool, or raise your rates without second-guessing yourself for weeks
- **Avoid regret**: Never take on a client or expense that creates a cash crunch two months later

### Social Needs
- **Appear decisive**: Be the founder who knows their numbers, not the one who says "I have to check with my accountant"
- **Earn team trust**: If you have contractors or employees, they need to know payroll won't be late
- **Credibility with advisors**: Investors, mentors, and accountants respect founders who understand their own cashflow

## Circumstances

### When the Job Emerges
- **Before hiring**: "Can I afford to bring on a contractor at $4K/month? What does that do to my runway?"
- **Before a slow season**: "I know December is quiet for me—do I have enough to cover expenses for 6 weeks?"
- **After a big payment lands**: "I just got paid $30K—how much of this is actually mine after taxes and overhead?"
- **When considering a price increase**: "If I raise my rates 20%, what does that do to my projected annual income?"
- **When a client churns**: "I just lost my biggest client—how long can I operate before I need to replace that revenue?"
- **When considering a tool or service**: "Is a $500/month hire or software investment actually affordable right now?"

### Constraints
- **No forward-looking view**: Most tools (QuickBooks, bank dashboards) show what happened; almost none show what's coming
- **Unpredictable income**: Variable and project-based revenue makes forecasting feel impossible without a tool that accounts for seasonality and deal pipelines
- **Mental accounting errors**: Owners confuse revenue with profit; they see $80K in the bank and think they're rich, not accounting for $30K in taxes and $15K in upcoming expenses
- **Decision latency**: By the time a freelancer figures out their cash position manually, the decision window has closed
- **Fear of being wrong**: Making a "data-driven" decision and still being wrong feels worse than not deciding—so owners avoid formal analysis altogether

## Steps (How People Currently Solve It, Solution-Independent)

1. **Decision trigger**: Opportunity or necessity arises (hire someone, raise prices, take on a new client)
2. **Mental estimate**: Quick gut check of bank balance vs. known upcoming expenses
3. **Spreadsheet (sometimes)**: Build a rough 3-month cashflow model in Google Sheets; takes 45–90 minutes
4. **Sanity check**: Ask a friend, partner, or accountant: "Does this seem reasonable to you?"
5. **Default to conservatism**: If uncertain, choose the safer option (don't hire, don't invest, don't raise prices)
6. **Revisit later**: Tell yourself "I'll do this when I have more visibility"—often never happens
7. **Regret (sometimes)**: Either missed a growth opportunity due to overcaution, or made a move that created a cash crunch

## Needs (How People Would Describe a Job Well Done)

### Core Needs
- **Show me a 90-day cashflow forecast** based on confirmed invoices, expected recurring payments, and typical expense patterns
- **Model a decision before I make it**: "If I add $4K/month in contractor costs starting next month, here's what my cashflow looks like"
- **Separate profit from revenue**: Show me what I actually keep after taxes and expenses—not just what I billed
- **Flag upcoming cash crunches** before they happen: "In 6 weeks, your projected balance drops below $5K—here's why"
- **Show me my break-even point** and how far I am from it

### Emotional Needs
- **Give me permission to say yes**: When the numbers are clear, I want to feel confident making a growth decision
- **Make the "no" feel rational, not fearful**: If I can't afford something, show me the data so I know it's a real constraint, not anxiety
- **Reduce the time I spend worrying about money**: If I trust my cashflow picture, I can focus on the work

### Data & Visibility Needs
- **Show income by source**: Which clients, projects, or products are driving profit—not just revenue
- **Track expense trends**: Am I spending more on tools, contractors, or overhead than I was 6 months ago?
- **Connect to tax obligations**: "After your Q3 tax payment of $8K, your effective cash balance will be $14K, not $22K"
- **Show scenario comparisons**: "Option A (hire now) vs. Option B (wait 60 days)—here's the cash difference"

## Customer Evidence

This job is validated across **all 20 interviews** spanning both the Invoicing & Collections and Tax & Compliance PM areas:

- **7 of 20** explicitly said they made a conservative business decision (delayed hire, skipped tool, turned down project) because they couldn't see their cash position clearly
- **5 of 20** described wishing they had a tool that would tell them what they could "actually afford"
- **12 of 20** expressed some version of mental accounting error—confusing bank balance with profit
- **4 of 20** have built manual cashflow models in Google Sheets; all described it as "tedious" and "something I let go stale"
- **6 of 20** said their single biggest financial goal was "to stop being reactive and start being proactive"

## Connected Hypotheses

This job drives several strategic bets in the decision-making layer roadmap:

- ✅ **Validated**: Profit-by-month visibility changes savings and hiring behavior—owners make bolder decisions when they trust the numbers
- ✅ **Validated**: Cashflow blindness causes conservatism, not just uncertainty—users actively delay growth decisions due to lack of visibility
- 🔄 **Backlog**: Scenario modeling ("what if I hire?") has higher decision impact than historical reporting alone
- 🔄 **Backlog**: Connecting tax liability to available cash changes how owners think about their bank balance
- ⚠️ **Challenged**: A 12-month forecast is too long for most solopreneurs—90 days is the actionable horizon for this ICP
