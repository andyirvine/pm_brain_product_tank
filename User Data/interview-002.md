## Background & Context

Raj founded his marketing agency 6 years ago and has grown it to $900K ARR with a team of 8. The agency works with 50+ active clients ranging from small e-commerce stores to mid-market SaaS companies. Revenue model is hybrid: recurring retainers (60% of revenue) and project-based work (40%).

Raj is a growth-focused founder. He's invested in systems and tools to scale, but was struggling with visibility into cashflow before adopting Ledger.

---

## The Problem: Invisible Invoice Management

### Before Ledger

**Raj's own words**: "With 50 clients, I had no idea which invoices were paid and which were sitting unpaid for 90 days. Ledger's dashboard solved that in literally 30 seconds. Now I see everything at a glance."

### The Specific Crisis That Led to Ledger

**Situation**: 
- Had a $5,000 invoice outstanding for 4 months
- Didn't realize it was unpaid because payment data was scattered
- Email confirmation from client lost; payment maybe wasn't sent; no one knew

**Discovery**:
- During Ledger onboarding, Raj uploaded invoice history
- Realized that **12% of his invoiced revenue was sitting unpaid**
- Some invoices were 30, 60, even 90 days past due

**Action taken**:
- Began systematic follow-up with all overdue invoices
- Collected **$25K in previously untracked revenue in 3 weeks**

**Impact**: This single action improved cash position and proved ROI of Ledger within weeks.

---

## Current Workaround (Pre-Ledger)

1. **Invoiced via Quickbooks** - Invoices generated and tracked in QB
2. **Payments scattered** - Clients paid via Stripe, ACH transfers, checks, PayPal
3. **Manual tracking** - CEO (Raj) maintained a Google Sheet: client name, invoice amount, date, payment status (manually updated)
4. **Monthly reconciliation** - Raj spent 3-4 hours/month matching invoices to payments

**Pain points**:
- Stripe + ACH payments came through with inconsistent memo lines
- Checks arrived without clear invoice reference
- Multiple spreadsheets in different states of updates
- No single source of truth

**Raj**: "I'd get a bank notification that $2,500 arrived, spend 15 minutes trying to figure out which client it was from, then have to update three different places."

---

## How Ledger Changed Behavior

### The Dashboard
"Ledger's main value is the dashboard. I open it and instantly see:
- Which invoices are paid
- Which are pending
- Which are overdue
- Who the client is
- How much is outstanding"

**Before**: Raj didn't know his own accounts receivable balance  
**Now**: Raj reviews dashboard weekly and knows exact outstanding balance, by client

### The Impact on Cashflow Predictability

**Key metric**: Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)
- **Before Ledger**: ~65 days
- **After Ledger (6 months)**: ~35 days
- **Improvement**: 46% reduction in collection time

How this happened:
1. Visibility revealed chronic payers (certain clients always 30+ days late)
2. Raj started proactive follow-up (instead of hoping)
3. Added language to contracts about payment expectations
4. Started auto-billing recurring clients on fixed schedule
5. Client payment behavior shifted once they realized Raj was tracking

### The Workflow Tax Reduction

Before Ledger, Raj spent:
- 3-4 hours/month on invoice reconciliation
- 30+ minutes per week chasing payments manually
- Unpredictable time handling payment disputes

After Ledger:
- 30 minutes/month on invoice review
- Automated reminders handle most follow-up
- Systematic overdue report (triggers payment chase)

**Time saved**: ~8 hours/month = 96 hours/year

## Segment-Specific Insight: Agency vs. Solopreneur

**Key difference from solopreneurs (Interviews 001, 003)**: 

Raj has a team. This changes the dynamic:
- **Solopreneurs**: Emotional reluctance to chase payment. Need automation to remove the personal discomfort.
- **Agencies**: No emotional barrier. The barrier is operational (visibility + delegation). Raj can tell a team member "Follow up on these 5 invoices" because he trusts them and payment chasing is just a job.

For Raj, **visibility is the solution**, not automation.
